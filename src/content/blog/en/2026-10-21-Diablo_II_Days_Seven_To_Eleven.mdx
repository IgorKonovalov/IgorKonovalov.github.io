## Stats and states

The second simulation phase was approved on the evening of the 16th and closed on the 17th at 20:06. It is the part of the game that adds things up: how an item's bonus reaches the wearer's strength, and how a timed state, a shrine's blessing, an aura, a curse, is applied, refreshed and expires. Eighteen sessions with six characters built by the tools, zero mismatches, and the counts are the kind a run of a tree produces: 891,844 stat reads in the logged windows, 232,042 cache writes, 17,634 requirement checks, 3,643 state applies, of which 2,608 were refreshes of a state already there, and 6,207 aura durations, every one of them the aura's delay plus one.

The static reading had claimed that no function in this phase draws a random number, and the plan turned the claim into a check: the seed logged on entry and exit of every event kind. One kind drew. A damaging aura, Sanctuary, takes _"one `RNG_RangeB` per hit"_, so it was checked by chaining instead, 746 pairs. On the 21st the catalogue read it narrower: one draw per run of the aura, whatever the number of units it hits, still inferred.

Three things in the spec that a player would not guess. An item's per-level bonus, the kind that grows with your character level, _"is not refreshed when the wearer levels up"_, 94 entries, confirmed; it is recomputed when the item is moved. The ethereal requirement discount of ten decided 30 of the requirement checks in the run, a character with strength 115 to 124 against a pair of War Boots that want 125. And a refresh of the whole tree runs every 136 frames with no player action at all; what queues it was not read. The community's guide says stat operation 6 _"does not work"_ and operation 7 updates only on the client; the server computes both, and no stock item reaches them, because the two affixes that use them cannot spawn. The review also struck a number from the reading, _"96 s at 25 frames a second"_ for a shrine, because the project's rule since day six is that timing is stated in frames and nothing else.

## Rooms that sleep

Day six's post did not mention room lifecycle because nobody had named it. The game puts a room to sleep when no player is near, and wakes it when one comes back, and the waking recreates monsters, objects and items from records it kept. Any reimplementation has to do this the same way, or a player who walks away and returns finds a different room. The plan was approved at 21:15 on the 16th, and its run on the 17th was the largest of the window: 13 sessions, 2,215,807 checks, 2,440 rooms released and 1,362 restored, 2,035 monsters brought back, every one of them with the life the model predicted.

The code reading was wrong in four places, three of them: An item restore takes two draws on the game seed, and a simple item comes back with a start seed of zero. A player's visit between two release passes does not reset the idle count. And the reading's headline surprise, a dead champion saved and restored alive, turned out unreachable: _"The save test's `0x18` mask is 'special or minion'. Every logged champion carries `0x08`, so a dead champion is never saved."_

Against the community, the lifecycle spec contradicts a belief players repeat often. A level without the `SaveMonsters` flag is said to leave its monsters _"injured"_; in 1.14d, _"any other live monster of a released room is freed without a record, and the room is not populated again, so it is gone, not injured."_ Blood Raven is said to be back at full health after twenty minutes; the rule is that a restored monster keeps its saved life if fewer than 7,500 frames passed between the save and the restore, and gets its maximum otherwise. And Prevent Monster Heal is said to reset when you walk too far: nothing resets by distance, _"a released and restored monster is a new unit."_

Two batches ran in the emulator rather than the game, the method ADR-0015 admitted on day six: one function of the binary at a time, in Ghidra's processor emulator, on inputs the batch chose, with every memory read traced. The first emulated 28,643 cases over ten families of functions and every one matched bit for bit, with a negative control failing as it should. The second added 18,800 and a `guard` directive, so that a case whose program counter reaches a fatal path stops instead of wandering. It also found a limit of the emulator itself: under its instruction model, dividing the smallest 32-bit integer by minus one returns zero where the real processor traps. _"It does not change a decision, so nothing goes to the owner."_

Two statements came back contradicted. The seeds spec had said that a seed of 2³¹ or more is given on the command line as its negative decimal. The run showed the parser copies a switch's text only up to the next dash, so `-seed -1` arrives empty, reads as zero, and the game takes the clock; a value of 2³¹ or more saturates. The seeds you can reproduce from the command line are 1 to `0x7FFFFFFF`. And the Cube spec said the cow portal's spot search draws; it does not, the portal object does, 171, 173 and 171 steps in the three openings. The treasure-class batch found the kind of overclaim a sweep exists for: the quality roll's clamp, which the step note said _"never acted in the runs"_, acts in 667 of the batch's rolls and decides 138. The note was true of the two quality runs it was written on, a week earlier.

## Game rules

The project's second decision, on day one, drew the scope around creation: items, monsters, levels. Day six brought in the simulation. On the evening of the 16th a third decision, ADR-0017, brought in what the ADR calls game rules, two areas, three rows on the map, outside the simulation's phase order: quest progression and travel, and the item economy. Its reason: _"A reimplementation that has every creation path but no quest progression, no waypoints and no prices is not a playable game, and those are the gaps a reimplementer meets first after the world generator."_ The evidence rule is by the shape of the claim. A one-shot function, a price, a repair cost, a gold amount, is reproduced as a creation path is; an event-driven change, a quest bit set by a kill, a durability point lost on a hit, is reproduced per event under the simulation's rule. And the ADR names the cost: _"Quests are hardcoded. There is no quest table; 1.14d inlines each quest's callbacks."_

### Quests and travel

Three runs, on the 17th, 18th and 19th: 11, 21 and 13 sessions, 45,714 checks, zero mismatches. The static reading of the quests had 596 symbols and the spec that came out of it is the most inferred on the map, 322 inferred against 117 confirmed, because every quest's callbacks are code, and there are a lot of callbacks that no session reached.

What the runs settled. A town portal takes three draws, not the five the reading said: _"Both ends come out of `SUnitCreate` already in their mode... so neither the creation nor the following `SetMode` draws."_ The door to the Arreat Summit reads one byte, quest 35's private "done in this game", and refused both characters built to test it, one without the word, one with every quest done. A handler of quest 11 writes a bit of quest 12's word while quest 11 is switched off, 44 writes. The stones of the Tristram cairn have to be touched in an order, and the order is a draw on the quest seed, made once per game at the first use of the decoded scroll. Tyrael's travel to Harrogath is not a line in his menu: the stock menu has no travel line, and the travel is the portal object beside him. And the hostility delay the community gives as ten seconds for leaving town: _"held for waypoints (10 000 ms), contradicted for portals: the portal gate waits 5000 ms."_

Two things about the method. The third run's review lowered the menu lines that had been confirmed from a screenshot: _"A screenshot is not enough for a `[C]`."_ And the night between the 18th and 19th was the first of the window's bad nights: the machine suspended for about nine hours, the game exited, and the driver kept clicking into the window that was no longer there until the main session killed it at 07:23. Each session now has a watchdog.

The first run reached no Hell session: _"the scripted walk to the town waypoint failed in five Hell launches."_ The third got two. No waypoint was activated from its dormant state, because every waypoint the sessions met was already active. And the boss steps of the quests, the kills that turn a quest's bits, were marked as candidates for me to play, with the note _"Still optional"_. I have not played them.

## Two players in one game

ADR-0008, the two-client harness, had been proposed since day two and accepted on the 16th, on one condition: before any session hosts a game, the lane makes sure that port 4000, the game's port, cannot be reached from outside the machine, and records how. That was my one root command of the window, on the 17th. The record quotes the output of `ufw status verbose` line by line, _"Default: deny (incoming)"_, the only allowed ports, and ends: _"No rule names 4000, no range contains it, and no rule is portless. So TCP 4000 is dropped on every non-loopback interface... This meets ADR-0008's condition."_ The lane's request had said _"Nothing else is asked: no probe, no second machine."_

The run itself was on the 20th: 19 sessions with two real clients on two harness slots, the joiner steered from the host's log because the joiner has no debugger, 417 party kills with drops, 46 vendor stock events with two players present, 385 experience shares, 12 quest bits written with both players in the game, 230 attacks of one player on another, every checker at zero mismatches. Four times a checker had to change, and each time the review ruled _"the checker, not the game, was wrong"_: one had hard-coded a single player. What the two clients found that one could not: the party experience share is computed with an 80-bit floating-point product of the share and the level, not rounded to 32 bits, and 98 inputs separate the two; the stock client refuses to declare hostility outside town; a dead player has a mode of his own, 17.

The review also found that the script which checked the firewall before each hosting session always exited zero, so the guard it was in would never have stopped a session. It is fail-closed now, and the rule says never to call it without `|| exit 1`. The re-tag raised 28 claims across eleven specs, the two-player cases the sweep had blocked fell from 14 to 7, and the plan closed at 15:23 on the 20th. Four cases that need two players at a boss were left for me, _"Not blocking anything."_

## Skills, in two plans

Phase three of the simulation is skills and missiles, and it is bigger than the first two together: 357 skills, 64 distinct start functions and 138 do functions on the server, 684 missile rows with their own do, hit and damage functions. ADR-0020, accepted on the 20th, splits it: _"Read literally, ADR-0014 would hold monster AI (phase 4) until every one of those functions has a reviewed spec."_ Plan 0045 is the machinery, dispatch, packets, missiles and event handlers, and its spec is the phase's spec; plan 0046 is the catalogue of every numbered function, and runs beside phase four without blocking it. On the same day I swapped phases four and five: animation and frame timing before monster AI, _"since skill and combat timing need frame counts first."_

The machinery's static phase closed on the 20th. The progressive finisher's seed step is a peek, saved and restored, not a draw. Against 61 community claims about skills and missiles, 27 held, 25 held with refinement, 5 were contradicted and 4 not covered, all of it read from the code and tagged inferred, with a caution in the finding: a contradiction from a community source _"may be a Resurrected change."_ The contradicted five, each now a case for the harness phase: a raised skeleton's level is not the caster's but `min(max(L × 3/4 + skill level, 1), L)`; a missile's velocity is `(VelLev × lvl) / 8 + Vel`, shifted left by eight, not `Vel` plus `VelLev` per level; `Accel` is added on every fifth path step, not every frame; `LevRange` has no minus one; and a missile that misses with pierce is freed at that unit, it does not keep flying.

The catalogue is 310 entries, and it runs in the emulator, because a function that creates a missile cannot be run in the game a thousand times with chosen inputs, and it can in the emulator. Fifteen batches; by the cut-off eleven were accepted, 230 of the 310 entries, 99,450 cases, zero mismatches. The Amazon batch's first session was killed by the out-of-memory handler before it checked; the kept run is the second.

The first batch produced a decision. Most catalogue functions end in a call whose inputs cannot be declared, a unit allocation, the path planner, a monster spawn, and the lane had stopped its cases at that call and claimed what happened before it. The review found that this _"does not meet ADR-0015 (1) and (3) as written"_, held the batch's claims at inferred, and drafted ADR-0021 for me: a case may end at an observed boundary, a named callee's entry, where the emulator stops before the first instruction and records the registers, the stack and the memory, and the claim covers _"nothing after the call."_ The rejected alternative was to read the old rule as already allowing it: _"widening an evidence rule by reading belongs in an ADR, not in a review."_ I accepted it at 14:56 on the 20th and the batch's claims went to confirmed without a re-run.

What the emulator found that the reading had not: the do function of Fend, Zeal and Fury _"returns 0 on every path"_; two start functions pass zero as the attack rating to the melee hit test, so Power Strike's to-hit bonus never reaches it; the elemental damage roll of one function is made once per run and not once per unit, which the stats spec had as "per hit"; Blade Fury's hit creates a missile of class 0, which is the arrow; the rings never test their step count, so a count of zero never ends. And one for the paladins: a function that Raise Skeleton and Revive run before anything else _"hits a player caster of class 3 for `maxhp / 8`"_, class 3 being the paladin in the class order, inferred: no case ran it with a paladin.

## Seed to world

Every spec in the core band was checked with oracles at its edges: a value read from the game instead of predicted, where the thing that produced it belonged to another spec. The treasure class run read the monster's seed from the log; the drop run read the item's. That was by design, ADR-0004, and it left a question the plans never asked: do the specs compose? Plan 0048 asks it. Its phase 0 inventoried 31 oracles across the core specs and assigned ten to be retired. Phase 1 retired eight: staff mods, the unit seed after creation, the preset window's monsters, one object function, the units of one placement path, another room's seed as chain state, gold items' seeds, an object's drop. Two it could not: one is read and still logged, one is named and not modelled. The tool that runs the chain is `tools/chain.py`.

Phase 2's bar is the first time this project has asked for the whole thing: _"2 000 room populations end to end from the game seed (no seed oracle in the window); 300 objects' creations; 100 special monsters...; 500 drops predicted from the dying unit's logged seed through TC, quality, affix, unique or set and property rolls with no builder oracle."_ Run 1 started on the 20th and ran through the night. One session was cut at 20:12 when the out-of-memory killer took the lane's agent and driver together and the game ran on without a pilot until its timer. Another sat through a seven-hour suspend and checked clean. The result: 28 logs, 26 passing, 156 levels of every generator type in every act and difficulty, 9,057 objects, 44 champion and 110 unique packs with 147 mod rolls, 849 monster drops with 951 items, 2,514 treasure class calls and 1,286 item creations, none failed. Two logs each had one mismatch in the objects model, and the lane's own explanation of them was wrong; the review's reading is that the two candidates are monsters re-created after their room was released and returned, _"the re-creations have no `CM` line and no modelled mark."_ Short of the bar: 1,222 of the 2,000 populations, which is about sixteen more sessions. Run 2 is on slot 1 now. The `chain` row was added to the core band at the phase 0 review, which is why the map has one row unreviewed.

The ledger has a purpose beyond counting: every few reviews the reviewer reads its recent rows and promotes any fix seen twice into a rule in the lane's own list, and drops a rule that has not recurred in thirty reviews. The lists exist now, one per lane skill, each rule with the lane it was seen in. From the reverser's: _"A new code reading is `[I]`. `[C]` needs a named second source."_ _"Recount every count from the binary with a kept command, never from memory or a partial scan."_ _"'Never', 'only', 'no X': say what the run separates. If one case carries both readings (Andariel has flag `0x08` and `boss`), that half stays `[I]`."_ And from the architect's own list, which it calls its blind spots: _"A claim settled at review gets the lane's scrutiny."_ _"Label a plan's estimates as estimates."_

The backlog changed shape twice. On the 17th it was one file of 730 lines and 519 entries, 464 of them struck, and in two days about 170 had been added and _"almost every lane merge conflicted in this file."_ ADR-0018 made it one file per entry, with no committed index, _"because a shared index would bring back the hot spot."_ Three days later the folder held 55 open and 80 closed entries, I said it was getting large, and ADR-0019 moved the closed ones to an archive. At the cut-off: 43 open, 186 archived.

And the nights. The 17th: a shutdown at about 21:38 that killed a Hell session mid-cycle. The 18th to 19th: the nine-hour suspend. The 19th, a Sunday: by ten in the morning three lanes had _"stopped at the owner's usage limit"_, my subscription's cap, and nothing landed after. The 20th: a debugger crash under Wine in the middle of a batch, a thread race in gdb 18.1; at 20:12 the memory manager killed every agent at once, no resume note; and from 23:17 to 06:20 the machine slept again, so the ledger books 428 of one lane's 624 wall-clock minutes as suspend. None of it lost a claim.
