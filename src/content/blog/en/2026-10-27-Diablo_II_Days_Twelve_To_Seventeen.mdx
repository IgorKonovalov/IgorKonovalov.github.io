## Skills

Plan 0045 is the machinery of skills and missiles: dispatch, packets, missiles, event handlers. The previous post had its static phase done and five community claims contradicted on paper. Phase 2 started on the 21st, was paused twice at my request, and ran on the 25th: 18 sessions with eight characters, 335,618 checks, zero mismatches. A second part on the 27th added 15 sessions, also zero, and re-checked the first part's 18 logs. The sessions are played by the pilot, the project's script that drives the game from screenshots, and a skill shows up in the logs as two functions: a start function, run when the cast begins, and a do function, run on its action frame.

The five contradictions, as the runs met them:

- **Raise Skeleton's level.** The community says a skeleton is raised at the caster's level. The code gives `min(max(L × 3/4 + skill level, 1), L)`. A caster of level 86 with the skill at 20 raised every skeleton at 84.
- **Missile range.** The community adds `LevRange` for each level beyond the first; the code multiplies it by the level. The case was Blaze, whose `LevRange` is 25, and the first part could not get it: _"the pilot teleports"_, so no walking trail of fire was logged. The second part walked: 271 Blaze creations at skill level 2, each with the code's range and not the community's, which counts from level two and would give one `LevRange` less.
- **Acceleration** is added on every fifth step of a missile's path, not on every frame: 1,657 accelerating steps, 278 of them fifth steps, 60 at the cap.
- **A piercing missile that misses its to-hit** keeps flying and does no damage, the community says. The code ends it at that unit.
- **Missile velocity**, `((VelLev × lvl) / 8 + Vel) << 8`, is the one the runs could not show. The monsters that fire the right missile were all met at level one, where the level term is zero: _"22 `spike2` creations … all at level 1."_ The ruling was not to send the pilot to Nightmare for quill rats; the Blood Moor ones stay an opportunistic check.

The run also corrected the reading in one place. The static spec had said that Raise Skeleton draws nothing from the caster's seed. The first dev sessions on the 21st saw the seed step once after the spawn, three times out of three, and the run on the 25th located the draw at `0x005C4430`.

_Fleshripper_ was the knife that broke the combat model a week earlier, 101 mismatches in a kill handler nobody had read. The handler model now replays all 3,461 old fires, that session's log included, and the second skills run gave a Barbarian the knife in one hand and _Lacerator_ in the other. One finding is about the client. A refused skill never reaches the server: _"the client runs the same `SKILLS_CanUse` on its own copy of the player every frame (`0x004D9FC5`) and sends no request for a refused skill"_, so the refusal cases are a shortfall that cannot be closed from the server's side. Another is about Kick. The stock Kick row has a minimum and maximum damage of zero, so its own hit does zero physical damage: 25 deliveries reached `COMBAT_ApplyDamage` _"with result 9, physical 0 … where `GetPhysMin`'s `Kick` branch would give at least 49 920."_ The strength-and-dexterity branch for kicks is real, but it is reached by the Assassin's Dragon Talon, Dragon Tail and Dragon Flight, and among monsters only the Shadow Warrior and the Shadow Master hold a skill with the kick bit.

## The catalogue, 310 of 310

Plan 0046, the catalogue of every numbered skill function, runs them one by one in Ghidra's processor emulator, as the previous post described. The last four batches ran on the 21st, 42,600 cases, zero mismatches, and Phase 1 closed at 310 of 310 entries. What the emulator showed:

- A monster function, do 152, _"is do 95 … instruction for instruction"_: 147 instructions, none different.
- Do 112 casts one of five fixed curses, chosen by the caster's seed modulo five. One of the five, Lower Resist, has a resistance branch that cannot be reached.
- The exploding and poison shrines drop five to nine potions. "Five to ten" is only the text of the shrines' editor column.
- Baal's tentacle draws two or three times, and which one depends on a local variable that its function _"never writes before the call … No emulation can give the value the game leaves there; only a logged cast can."_ The claim stays inferred and goes to the harness phase, which needs Baal.

## Animation and frame timing

Phase four was approved on the 25th, in the order I had set on the 20th: animation and frame timing before monster AI, _"since skill and combat timing need frame counts first."_ Plan 0050's first phase read the animation data: `animdata.d2` is 256 hash blocks of 160-byte records, and the expansion's file wins. Another file, `eanimdata.d2`, is named by no string in the binary at all. Monster sequence events agree with their animation record in only 81 of 805 steps.

The reading of the code says something about how the game runs: _"most server modes are not advanced frame by frame; `SUNIT_ScheduleModeEvents` queues the frame events (codes 1–4) and the mode end at the start."_ The rates are one function with nine branches, and the bonus of faster cast, hit recovery, block and so on is the diminishing formula `B = k·s / (k + s)`, with k 120, and 150 for run and walk, confirmed over 3,413 emulated cases. And an edge case: _"a negative factor (FCR ≤ −55, FHR or FBR ≤ −36) wraps to the maximum rate."_

The community has a lot to say about breakpoints, so the comparison was large: 61 claims from the Maxroll and Basin pages, the old run and walk thread for 1.09, the 1.13 movement clarification and PureDiablo's tables. They were filed blind, before the lane read the code.

The breakpoint tables themselves came out right: _"every change point equal (78/78)."_ The frame count in 76 of them is one less than the code's, and the review ruled that a counting difference: the code's count is one more than the tables'. What was contradicted, among others. Against claims about 1.14d itself: the attack factor is clamped between 15 and 175, while hit recovery and block are not capped at all; a knockback on a player ends in neutral with no hit recovery; and the block gate is a cooldown, `frame − last block > ⌊stat102 / 8⌋ + 15`, where the community says there is none. Against Resurrected sources: Double Swing's base is 70, not the 120 Maxroll gives, and the cast frames of the Druid, the Necromancer, the Werewolf and the Werebear all differ. Against the 1.09 thread: Charge moves at 2.5 times the base speed, not 1.5, and the run factor of "65" _"matches only read as hex"_. 0x65 is 101.

The first run of the phase, on the 27th: 55 sessions, zero mismatches, 298,373 rate calls, 99,241 movement steps. The Fallen showed the classic monster's special modes taking the moving rate, 1,266 calls. Blood Raven in Hell knocked the player back 13 times and 12 left him in neutral in the same frame. Frozen Orb's next cast came at the do frame plus the delay, 72 of 73. The shortfall is get-hits: _"every fragile character died after 2–9: a get-hit needs a hit of a sixteenth of life."_

## Monster AI, and a sixth phase

Plan 0052 was drafted on the 25th, held, re-drafted on the 27th, and approved by me at 17:34. The eighth I answered against the recommendation, which the plan records as such: _"Catalogue coverage: the whole AI table (differs from the recommendation). The `aidist` field is set only on Mephisto and his Uber copy. And the review found something the phase had not looked for: a second AI table at `0x0073D358`, eighteen rows of four words, ten of them holding functions no row of the first table has.

The same approval accepted ADR-0022. The project's phases end at monster AI, and the ADR adds one after it, the server frame: _"the game tick (`GameTick` and every pass it makes), the per-unit update order … the event queue … the timers … and the room activation order within a frame."_ Its evidence is new too, a frame window: _"a stated run of consecutive frames in a small scene … in which every in-scope event of every unit is logged and every entry seed is asserted, none resynchronised."_ Everything so far has checked a function's output from its logged inputs; a frame window checks that nothing in between was missed. The ADR names its risk: _"The order may be partly non-deterministic under wine."_ My note of the morning had said that phase five is the last of the old scope and anything beyond needs a scope ADR. ADR-0022 is that ADR.

## The gate that stopped guarding

The project has a gate, `tools/gate.py`, that checks the documents against each other on every commit: a spec's tag counts, cross-references, symbols nobody mentions. The commit hook runs it with a timeout of 20 seconds, and the hook is deliberately lenient: _"A broken gate never blocks work … a timeout … passes with a one-line warning."_ I asked for the gate to be made fast, and plan 0053 found what the leniency had meant in practice: _"the fast tier took 153 to 183 s, of which 151 s was the W3 loop (about 50 000 `re.search` calls over the joined corpus), so the commit hook's 20 s timeout failed open."_ W3 is the check for a symbol of `re/symbols.csv` that no spec or finding names. With 3,666 symbols it ran each one as a regular expression over everything. Every commit had been passing with a one-line warning, for how long the records do not say. With a word set instead, the staged gate takes 5.6 s and W3 gives the same zero warnings, and the hook enforces again. It also added rules for things the reviews kept fixing by hand: a table row with more cells than its header, a map status that does not match the spec's front matter, a plans index that does not match the plan.
